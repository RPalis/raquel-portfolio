MVP Design Plan

What the Business Wanted to Validate:

LAFISE Bank is launching a new mobile banking MVP for its Central American markets. Before committing to development, the product team needed to validate that real users could complete core banking tasks without friction — and identify critical blockers before they reach production.


🎯 Research Objectives
• Validate hypotheses on digital payment needs
• Understand the priorities and language of the merchant user
• Identify biases from previous experiences with other apps


📊 Key Characteristics
• Entrepreneurs with a minimum of 6 months of experience
• Do not qualify for traditional bank card terminals
• Pay high fees on POS or are unaware of alternatives like Poket

• Research Brief

• Methodology

Research Approach

20 Participants
5 User Groups
45m Session Length
100% Moderated

Test Format

Moderated usability testing with think-aloud protocol. Conducted remotely and in-person across Costa Rica and Panama.

User Groups

Young Professionals (22–30), Mid-Career (31–45), University Students (18–22), Senior Users (55+), Unbanked Merchants.

Prototype

High-fidelity clickable Figma prototype covering 5 core flows: Onboarding, Transfer, Payment Request, Dashboard, Account Settings.

Metrics Captured

Task completion rate, time-on-task, error rate, SUS score, NPS, and qualitative insights.

Recording

Screen + audio recording with consent. Sessions transcribed and tagged using affinity mapping.

Analysis Method

Rainbow spreadsheet analysis, SUS scoring per group, priority severity matrix (impact × frequency).

Usability Testing Findings

5 Critical Findings From MVP Testing

62.5 SUS Score · Marginal
F1

Navigation Confusion

85% of participants couldn't locate 'Request Payment' without guidance. Tab labels were ambiguous — users expected task-based language, not category names.

F2

Transfer Friction

The money transfer flow required 7 steps. 70% of users expected fewer taps, influenced by WhatsApp Pay and Sinpe Móvil. Fee disclosure only appeared at step 6.

F3

Auth Overload

Biometric + PIN + OTP on the same session felt excessive to 65% of users — particularly seniors (55+), 40% of whom abandoned during authentication.

F4

Error Handling

Generic error messages caused anxiety. 55% of users abandoned after the first error screen with no clear recovery path or retry option visible.

F5 · Positive

Dashboard Clarity

Balance visibility praised by 80% of users. The transaction list was clear and familiar — the strongest-performing section and a validated foundation to build on.

Design Decisions

How We Solved It

01

IA Redefinition

The tabs Mi Pocket and Mi Cuenta are now tab bar main app entry points. Profile now lives in the top-right corner. Invoices and charges are the main CTAs. Smart filters for complex data search.

02

Tab Bar Redesign

Mi Pocket and Mi Cuenta are now the tab bar's main app entry points — task-based navigation that matches user mental models directly.

03

Smart Auth Tiers

Biometric-only for transfers under $50. PIN + biometric for $50–$500. Full OTP for amounts above $500. Risk-proportional security.

04

App Tour

An app tour was created to facilitate user onboarding on the main app features — reducing first-session drop-off and improving discoverability.

05

Transfer Flow 7→4 Steps

Consolidated recipient selection + amount + fee disclosure into a single smart screen. Confirmation simplified to one tap.

06

UI Redesign & Real Estate Optimisation

Introduced tiered membership UI with clear perks per level. Gamification mechanics ('earn & spend') added to transaction confirmations.

Iterations

IA Redefinition / Tab Bar Redesign / UI Redesign and Real Estate Optimisation and App Tour

Summary of Iterations and Recommendations

After final iteration a book of best design best practices and UX principles with the optimisations, findings, and UX flows were produced in order for the design team to create the final UI KIT, design system and refined flows to dev handoff.
